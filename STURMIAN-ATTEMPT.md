# Mechanical schedules and a growth–complexity obstruction

This note closes the mechanical-halving family left open at the end of
`APERIODIC-ATTEMPT.md`. It also excludes a specific aperiodic substitution
word. It does **not** settle Collatz: general itineraries need not belong to
either family, and no coverage theorem is supplied. The family exclusions are
written proofs. Their finite positive-integer collision criterion is now
kernel-checked in [`CollatzRepetition.lean`](CollatzRepetition.lean); the
word-complexity, rational-extension, and asymptotic arguments remain written
proofs. No novelty claim is made.

## 1. An exact finite collision criterion

Use the shortcut map

\[
 U(x)=\begin{cases}(3x+1)/2,&x\text{ odd},\\x/2,&x\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]

The following argument also works for a rational two-adic starting value
\(x_0=X_0/b\), where \(X_0\in\mathbb Z\) and \(b>0\) is odd. Keep this
denominator fixed. Numerators follow

\[
 X_{t+1}=\begin{cases}(3X_t+b)/2,&X_t\text{ odd},\\X_t/2,&X_t\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{1}
\]

Both branches are integer-valued on the indicated parity classes. Let
\(p(m)\) count distinct length-\(m\) factors in their infinite parity word.

**Finite criterion.** If

\[
 2\max_{0\le t\le p(m)}|X_t|<2^m,                              \tag{2}
\]

then the orbit repeats.

**Proof.** Among the \(p(m)+1\) blocks beginning at positions
\(0,\ldots,p(m)\), two agree, say at \(s<t\). Their affine constants
cancel, giving, for their common number \(j\) of odd letters,

\[
 2^m(X_{t+m}-X_{s+m})=3^j(X_t-X_s).
\]

Thus \(2^m\mid X_t-X_s\). By (2) its absolute value is smaller than
\(2^m\), so it is zero. Determinism gives an eventually periodic orbit.

For positive integers, `parity_gap_divisible`, `collision_of_small_gap`,
and `parity_collision_periodic` in the Lean file check these exact steps.
`factor_catalog_forces_repeat` additionally proves the finite pigeonhole
argument: a catalog of P parity words and P+1 starting states below `2^m`
force a repeated state among those positions. It assumes both the catalog
coverage and the height bounds explicitly; it does not assert they hold
for every Collatz orbit.

Consequently, if \(|X_t|\le Cg^t\), \(g>1\), and along arbitrarily large
\(m\)

\[
 p(m)\le\kappa m+C_0,\qquad g^\kappa<2,                      \tag{3}
\]

then the orbit repeats. A fixed finite prefix changes only constants.

The universal bound
\(|X_t|+b\le(3/2)^t(|X_0|+b)\) follows directly from (1). For positive
integer Collatz trajectories, the resulting threshold
\(\liminf p(m)/m\ge\log 2/\log(3/2)\) in the nonrepeating case is the
argument underlying Dubickas's Theorem 5. Its hypotheses concern a divergent
trajectory; they do not assert that one exists. The published proof is in
Section 5 of [Dubickas, *On integer sequences generated by linear maps*,
Glasgow Mathematical Journal 51 (2009), 243–252](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C40C0C07FEC20797475BB2899C436C9A/S0017089508004655a.pdf/on_integer_sequences_generated_by_linear_maps.pdf).

## 2. All irrational mechanical parity words are excluded

A lower or upper mechanical word has letters

\[
 v_t=\lfloor(t+1)\delta+\tau\rfloor-\lfloor t\delta+\tau\rfloor,
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 v_t=\lceil(t+1)\delta+\tau\rceil-\lceil t\delta+\tau\rceil,
 \qquad 0<\delta<1.                                           \tag{4}
\]

For each \(m\), these words have at most \(m+1\) factors of length \(m\).
Here is the finite counting reason. For lower words, normalize the phase at
a factor's start to \(\theta\in[0,1)\). Its cumulative counts are
\(\lfloor j\delta+\theta\rfloor\), \(1\le j\le m\). They can change
only at the at most \(m\) thresholds where one of these expressions crosses
an integer. Endpoints take the value on the interval to their right, so they
do not add isolated patterns. For upper words instead use
\(\theta=\lceil t\delta+\tau\rceil-(t\delta+\tau)\in[0,1)\) and
the cumulative counts \(\lceil j\delta-\theta\rceil\); the same bound
holds, including threshold phases.

The universal growth bound and (3), with \(\kappa=1\), force repetition
for every rational candidate \(X_0/b\). Repetition makes the parity word
eventually periodic and hence gives it a rational limiting density. But the
telescoping counts in (4), divided by their length, tend to \(\delta\).

**Theorem.** An irrational mechanical parity word, with any intercept, is
not the itinerary of any rational two-adic starting value. This also excludes
every shifted such word and every orbit with such a tail.

This statement concerns rationality, not the stronger assertion that the
binary expansion of the conjugacy value has full factor complexity.

## 3. The exact bridge from halving schedules

Suppose the accelerated odd exponents are prescribed by

\[
 h_i=\lfloor(i+1)\lambda+\rho\rfloor-
     \lfloor i\lambda+\rho\rfloor,
 \qquad \lambda\ge1,\quad 0\le\rho<1.                        \tag{5}
\]

Their partial sums are \(H_i=\lfloor i\lambda+\rho\rfloor\). In the
shortcut orbit starting at an odd value, its odd positions are exactly
\(H_0,H_1,H_2,\ldots\): an accelerated step of exponent \(h_i\) contributes
the parity block \(1\,0^{h_i-1}\).

Let \(S_t\) count odd positions strictly before integer time \(t\ge0\).
Since

\[
 H_i<t\iff i\lambda+\rho<t,
\]

we get the exact formula, including \(t=0\),

\[
 S_t=\left\lceil\frac{t-\rho}{\lambda}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 v_t=S_{t+1}-S_t.                                             \tag{6}
\]

Thus the shortcut parity word is upper mechanical with slope
\(\delta=1/\lambda\) and intercept \(-\rho/\lambda\). This is a
change of coding, not an identification of the two slopes.

**Corollary.** Every irrational \(\lambda\ge1\) in (5), at every
intercept, has no positive integer realization. The result remains true
after any finite initial orbit segment. In particular it excludes every
irrational subcritical slope
\(1<\lambda<\log_2 3\), which survived the previous note's multiplier
and packing bounds.

The whole subcritical family, including rational \(\lambda\), is excluded
as well. Equations (4)–(6) force an eventual cycle. Every positive accelerated
cycle has mean halving exponent strictly larger than \(\log_2 3\), because

\[
 2^{\sum h_i}=3^q\prod_{i<q}\left(1+\frac1{3n_i}\right)>3^q.
\]

But (5) has limiting mean \(\lambda\le\log_2 3\). The endpoint
\(\lambda=1\) is the all-ones parity word; repeated agreement forces
\(2^t\mid n_0+1\) for every \(t\), also impossible for a positive seed.

## 4. An explicit substitution family beyond Sturmian words

Let \(w\) be the fixed point beginning in 1 of

\[
 \sigma(1)=110,\qquad\sigma(0)=011.                           \tag{7}
\]

No rational two-adic orbit has parity word \(w\), any shift of \(w\),
or a tail equal to such a shift. The following supplies all needed bounds.

Every aligned block in (7) contains two odd letters. Under (1), its exact
three-step map is respectively

\[
 X\longmapsto\frac{9X+5b}{8},\qquad
 X\longmapsto\frac{9X+10b}{8}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 |X_{3j}|+10b\le(9/8)^j(|X_0|+10b),\qquad
 |X_t|\le C(9/8)^{t/3}.                                     \tag{8}
\]

At most two intermediate steps are absorbed into \(C\). A shift of the
word requires discarding at most two initial letters to recover alignment.

Choose the least \(r\ge0\) with \(m\le3^r\). Every length-\(m\)
factor lies in \(\sigma^r(ab)\) for one of at most four adjacent letter
pairs, beginning at one of \(3^r\) offsets. Therefore

\[
 p(m)\le4\cdot3^r<12m.                                      \tag{9}
\]

Together (8)–(9) satisfy (3), since

\[
 \left((9/8)^{1/3}\right)^{12}=(9/8)^4
 =6561/4096<2.
\]

It remains to rule out eventual periodicity of \(w\), rather than presume
it. Its letters obey

\[
 w_{3j}=w_j,\qquad w_{3j+1}=1,\qquad w_{3j+2}=1-w_j.
\]

An eventual period congruent to 1 modulo 3 would make \(w_j\) eventually
all zero, by comparing positions \(3j+1\) with their translates. One
congruent to 2 would make it eventually all one. Both conclusions contradict
the displayed recurrences. A period divisible by 3 yields a period one third
as large using \(w_{3j}=w_j\). Repeated division reaches one of the two
contradictions. This proves the required aperiodicity and completes the
exclusion, including signed rational numerators.

## 5. Source audit and exact limits

López–Stoll's paper distinguishes the two-adic conjugacy value from an
associated real-valued function. Its Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 give two-adic
expansions for intercept-zero mechanical words. Section 4's irrationality
statements concern the real-valued functions. They do not, by themselves,
identify the real and two-adic limits or prove that the conjugacy images have
full complexity. The latter is suggested by their finite examples, not
established there. This note's rationality exclusion instead uses the finite
integer collision argument above. See [López–Stoll, *The 3x+1 Conjugacy Map
over a Sturmian Word*, Integers 9 (2009), 141–162](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/j13/j13.pdf).

A September 2026 primary-source repository also contains a stronger
classification of positive **rational-slope** mechanical itineraries: only
the natural seeds 1 and 2 occur globally. Its inspected declarations
`mechanical_reaches_one` and `mechanical_classification` assume only
positivity and equality with the specified mechanical itinerary. The
dependency chain is `RationalMechanical` → `RationalGrid` →
`MechanicalPeriod` → `MechanicalComplexity` and `EndpointCycles`. The first
three source files were inspected; the final two could not be fetched in this
pass. No independent local compilation or complete dependency audit of that
external development is claimed. See its [classification source](https://github.com/msharpe248/collatz/blob/main/lean/Collatz/RationalMechanical.lean)
and [paper source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/msharpe248/collatz/main/paper/mechanical_classification.tex).

Its rational-cycle mechanism can also be checked algebraically. A reduced
rational rotation visits phases 0 and \(q-1\); the corresponding period
words are \(0u1\) and \(1u0\). Coprimality makes their interior letters
identical. If both are return words, put \(L=|u|\), let \(r\) count its
ones, and write its affine map as \((3^rX+d)/2^L\). For the positive return
seeds \(x\) and \(y\) of \(1u0\) and \(0u1\), respectively,

\[
 D x=3^r+2d,\quad D y=6d+2^{L+1},\quad
 D=2^{L+2}-3^{r+1}>0.
\]

Hence \(D(3x-y+1)=2^{L+1}\). The odd positive integer \(D\) must equal
1. If \(L\ge1\), reduction modulo 8 contradicts
\(2^{L+2}-3^{r+1}=1\). Thus \(L=r=d=0\), giving \(x=1,y=2\).
The finite rational-phase coding underlying this reduction appears explicitly
in the inspected [RationalGrid source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/msharpe248/collatz/main/lean/Collatz/RationalGrid.lean).

The proved exclusions are substantial prescribed families. They do not give
an upper complexity bound for arbitrary Collatz itineraries, or establish
the strict growth–complexity inequality (3) for every hypothetical divergent
orbit. That missing universal premise is the remaining gap in this approach.
