# A necessary distance from every mechanical cycle word

**Result:** the parity word of any nontrivial primitive positive integer
Collatz cycle, with length N and p ones, must differ from every cyclic
rational mechanical word of slope p/N in at least **64 positions**.
The comparators are exactly the words defined in Section 1; arbitrary finite
prefixes of mechanical sequences with another slope are not covered.
Equivalently, at least **32
ones must be moved**: 32 removals and 32 insertions. The allowed moves have
arbitrary distances; they are not required to be adjacent.

This excludes every cycle within half-Hamming distance 31 of such a mechanical
word, at every period. It extends the
[single adjacent-swap result](MECHANICAL-SWAP-EXCLUSION.md).
It does not exclude arbitrary cycles or divergent orbits.

The proof combines a published logarithm theorem, written combinatorial and
analytic arguments, and Lean-checked finite arithmetic and checker soundness.
**The complete argument is not assembled into a Lean theorem.** No novelty
or computational record claim is made.

## 1. Setup and two opposing size bounds

Use the shortcut map
\[
 U(x)=\begin{cases}x/2&x\text{ even},\\(3x+1)/2&x\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
Let \(w\) be the parity word of a positive cycle of least period \(N\),
containing \(p\) ones. Its \(N\) states are distinct. There is no positive
fixed point; \(N=2\) gives only \(1\leftrightarrow2\), so assume \(N\ge3\).
The cycle equation gives \(0<p<N\) and
\[
 D=2^N-3^p>0,\qquad \delta=D/2^N.
\]

Choose any same-count mechanical comparator
\[
 b_r(i)=\left\lfloor\frac{(i+1)p+r}{N}\right\rfloor
       -\left\lfloor\frac{ip+r}{N}\right\rfloor,\qquad 0\le r<N.
\]
Let \(h\) be half the Hamming distance between \(w\) and \(b_r\).
The comparator need not be primitive, and no coprimality assumption is made.
We will contradict \(h\le31\).

For a word \(v\), write \(J_v(i)=\sum_{j<i}v_j\). Every cyclic rotation of
the comparator satisfies \(J_b(i)>ip/N-1\). Changing \(h\) ones into zeros
and \(h\) zeros into ones loses at most \(h\) ones in any prefix:
\[
 J_w(i)>ip/N-h-1.
\]
The affine numerator at each rotation is
\[
 \Phi(w)=\sum_{i:w_i=1}2^i3^{p-J_w(i)-1}.
\]
Since \(D>0\), each summand is less than \(3^h2^N\).
Thus every shortcut state satisfies
\[
 x<\frac{3^hp}{\delta}\le\frac{3^{31}p}{\delta}. \tag{1}
\]
Rotating both words preserves their distance, so (1) bounds all states.

A mechanical word has at most \(m+1\) factors of length \(m\). At most
\(2hm\) cyclic windows meet a changed site. Hence
\[
 P_w(m)\le(2h+1)m+1\le63m+1.
\]
Set \(m=\lfloor(N-2)/63\rfloor\). Then \(P_w(m)<N\). Two distinct cycle
states therefore share their first \(m\) parity bits and are congruent
modulo \(2^m\), so the cycle diameter is at least \(2^m\).
The generic collision implication is kernel checked in
[CollatzRepetition.lean](../CollatzRepetition.lean).
The case \(m=0\) uses the single empty factor and gives diameter at least 1.

Combining the diameter with (1) yields the necessary condition
\[
 \boxed{\delta<3^{31}p\,2^{-\lfloor(N-2)/63\rfloor}.} \tag{2}
\]
The same derivation works with 31 replaced by any fixed distance budget \(H\).

## 2. An effective period bound

Matveev's rational-product theorem gives
\[
 \log\delta>-C(1+\log N),\qquad C=602494200. \tag{3}
\]
The inputs are the positive rationals \(3,2\), nonzero integer exponents
\(p,-N\), and exponent bound \(N\). Unique factorization makes the product
difference nonzero. The constant follows from
\[
 1.4\cdot30^5\cdot2^{4.5}\log2\log3
 <1.4\cdot30^5\cdot23\cdot0.7\cdot1.1=C.
\]
The primary-source restatement is Theorem 2.1 of
[Languasco, Luca, Moree and Togbé, *Sequences of integers generated by two
fixed primes*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12188-025-00293-9).
This is an external mathematical input, not a new local Lean axiom.

For convenience, a common cutoff works for every \(H\le100\). Applying the
factor bound with \(m=\lfloor(N-2)/201\rfloor\), the height bound with
\(3^{100}\), and (3) would require
\[
 G(N):=\left(\frac{N-2}{201}-1\right)\log2
       -100\log3-\log N-C(1+\log N)<0.
\]
At \(N_0=10^{13}\), the elementary estimates
\(\log2>2/3,\ \log3<2,\ \log10<5/2\) imply
\[
 G(N_0)>\frac{2\cdot10^{13}}{603}-234-34C>0.
\]
The last comparison is exactly checked in Lean. Also
\[
 G'(N)>\frac2{603}-\frac{C+1}{10^{13}}>0
 \qquad(N\ge10^{13}).
\]
For the logarithm estimate, \(e^{5/2}\) exceeds its first five Taylor terms,
whose sum is \(4169/384>10\). We conclude
\[
 N<10^{13}. \tag{4}
\]

## 3. Exact rational separation reduces the period to 12287

Put \(\alpha=\log2/\log3\). The finite sums and remainder bounds in
[the earlier note, Section 5](MECHANICAL-SWAP-EXCLUSION.md#5-rational-separation-reduces-the-bound-to-255)
give explicit rational numbers \(L<\alpha<V\).
[MechanicalLogBracket.lean](../lean/MechanicalLogBracket.lean) now checks
\[
 \frac{5409303924479}{8573543875303}+2^{-98}
 <L<\alpha<V<
 \frac{6162414764360}{9767196315401}. \tag{5}
\]
The comparisons with \(\alpha\) use the written analytic remainder proof;
the Lean file checks the finite rational comparisons.

For the outer fractions \(A/B<C'/E\), Lean checks
\[
 BC'-AE=1,\qquad B+E=18340740190704>10^{13}.
\]
Every rational \(p/N\) strictly between these neighbors has \(N\ge B+E\):
the positive integers \(u=pB-AN\), \(v=C'N-pE\) obey \(Bv+Eu=N\).
This remains true for unreduced fractions.

Since \(D>0\) gives \(p/N<\alpha\), (4) and (5) force \(p/N\le A/B\).
Therefore
\[
 \lambda=N\log2-p\log3>2^{-98},\qquad
 \delta=1-e^{-\lambda}>\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}>2^{-100}. \tag{6}
\]
Here \(\log3>1\), and \(e^\lambda>1+\lambda\).

For \(12288\le N\le12350\), however,
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{N-2}{63}\right\rfloor\ge195,\qquad
 3^{31}<2^{50},\qquad p<N<2^{14}.
\]
Equation (2) would give \(\delta<2^{50+14-195}=2^{-131}\), contradicting
(6). Increasing \(N\) by 63 increases the floor exponent by one and
multiplies \(N2^{-\lfloor(N-2)/63\rfloor}\) by
\((N+63)/(2N)<1\). The first block covers every residue modulo 63.
Consequently
\[
 3\le N<12288. \tag{7}
\]

## 4. The finite count-pair disjunction

For every \(N\) in (7) and every \(p\) with \(3^p<2^N\),
[MechanicalDistanceFinite.lean](../lean/MechanicalDistanceFinite.lean),
theorem finite_31, proves
\[
 \boxed{
 3^{31}p2^N\le D\,2^{\lfloor(N-2)/63\rfloor}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 p2^N\le3000000D.} \tag{8}
\]
The first branch contradicts (2).

The checker verifies one maximal eligible count \(q\) per length, including
both inequalities \(3^q<2^N\le3^{q+1}\). Kernel proofs show that every
eligible \(p\) is at most \(q\), and that each branch of (8) is preserved
when \(q\) decreases to \(p\). Numerators decrease and denominators increase.
Thus the check covers every count, not just counts near the critical slope.
The candidate generator need not be proved correct because every candidate
is independently checked.

Only the finite calculation uses native_decide. No word enumeration or
floating-point comparison enters this certificate.

## 5. The second branch forces a checked small state

Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_p\) be the odd states of the shortcut cycle and
\(M=\min_i x_i>0\). Multiplying all step equations gives
\[
 2^N=\prod_{i=1}^p\left(3+\frac1{x_i}\right).
\]
Hence
\[
 \delta=1-\prod_{i=1}^p\left(1-\frac1{3x_i+1}\right)
 \le\sum_{i=1}^p\frac1{3x_i+1}
 \le\frac{p}{3M+1}<\frac{p}{3M}. \tag{9}
\]
The finite-product inequality follows by induction from
\(1-(1-a)(1-b)=a+b-ab\le a+b\), for \(0\le a,b\le1\).
It follows that \(M<p/(3\delta)\).

The second branch of (8) gives \(p/\delta\le3000000\), so
\[
 M<1000000. \tag{10}
\]

[BoundedStandardCycle.lean](../lean/BoundedStandardCycle.lean) independently
checks that **every start from 1 through 1,000,000 reaches 1** in fewer than
200,000 ordinary steps. Its direct checker has no escape-window branch:
exhaustion returns false, and only reaching 1 returns true.
Kernel soundness turns this finite computation into a reachability theorem.

The file also proves the ordinary-to-shortcut implication, periodic
reduction, and that a shortcut cycle containing 1 contains only 1 and 2.
Its theorem shortcut_cycle_small_start_is_trivial therefore excludes any
nontrivial cycle having a state as small as (10), regardless of the sizes
of its other states. This contradicts the second branch of (8).

Both branches are impossible. Thus \(h\le31\) is impossible for a
nontrivial primitive cycle, proving the stated distance bound.

## 6. What remains open

This is a necessary condition on every nontrivial primitive cycle, not a
proof that no such cycle exists. Distance from mechanical words can grow
with the period; we have no universal upper bound on that distance.
Aperiodic divergence is also untouched by this cycle argument.

The same finite test with threshold one million does not close distance
budget 32. Lean checks that both branches fail at \((N,p)=(4701,2966)\).
This demonstrates a limitation of these inequalities; it does not assert
that an integer cycle with those counts exists. Increasing the checked
range might improve the numerical distance bound, but alone cannot
settle unbounded distance or all positive starts.

## Verification scope

The [verification record](../results/mechanical-distance/verification.json)
binds source hashes, compiler version, commands, and logs. The finite
count-pair, rational-comparison, and direct-convergence checks use
native_decide, which trusts Lean's compiler and native runtime.
Their soundness and cycle-reduction proofs use standard logical axioms.

The height and factor-count estimates, affine interpretation, product
identity and inequality, real logarithms, Matveev application, and their
assembly into the all-period exclusion remain written arguments. The
certificate does not claim to prove the full Collatz conjecture.
