# Cumulative odd density, growth, and parity complexity

This note gives necessary conditions for a hypothetical divergent positive
Collatz orbit. It strengthens the complexity restriction when cumulative odd
density or cumulative growth is bounded. It supplies no universal upper
bound on itinerary complexity and does not settle Collatz. The arguments
below are written proofs; no new Lean compilation is claimed. No novelty
claim is made.

## 1. Definitions and the bounded correction

Let \(x_t=U^t(x_0)\), \(x_0>0\), for the shortcut map

\[
 U(x)=\begin{cases}(3x+1)/2,&x\text{ odd},\\x/2,&x\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]

Assume throughout Sections 1–4 that the orbit never repeats. Equivalently,
it is unbounded; distinct positive integer states also imply
\(x_t\to\infty\). Put

\[
 v_t=x_t\bmod2,\qquad J_t=\sum_{i<t}v_i,\qquad
 D(t)=J_t\log3-t\log2,\qquad
 E(T)=\max_{0\le t\le T}D(t).
\]

All logarithms are natural. Let \(p(m)\) be the number of distinct
length-\(m\) factors of the full infinite parity word \(v\).

The exact identity is

\[
 x_t=x_0e^{D(t)}P_t,\qquad
 P_t=\prod_{\substack{i<t\\v_i=1}}
       \left(1+\frac1{3x_i}\right).                         \tag{1}
\]

The packing proof in [`APERIODIC-ATTEMPT.md`](APERIODIC-ATTEMPT.md),
Sections 2–3, establishes \(\sum_t1/x_t\le1920\). Consequently

\[
 1\le P_t\uparrow P_\infty\le e^{640},\qquad
 \log x_t=D(t)+\log(x_0P_\infty)+o(1).                      \tag{2}
\]

In particular \(D(t)\to\infty\). We use the existing packing argument
only for its bounded correction, not an assertion about the odd density of
every shifted block.

## 2. The finite inequality behind the asymptotics

Among the \(p(m)+1\) parity blocks starting at times
\(0,\ldots,p(m)\), two agree, say at \(s<t\). Cancelling their affine
constants gives

\[
 2^m(x_{t+m}-x_{s+m})=3^j(x_t-x_s)
\]

for their common odd-letter count \(j\). Hence
\(2^m\mid x_t-x_s\). The difference is nonzero by nonrepetition, and
both states are positive, so

\[
 2^m\le|x_t-x_s|<\max_{0\le i\le p(m)}x_i
 \le x_0P_\infty e^{E(p(m))}.
\]

Writing \(C=\log(x_0P_\infty)\), we obtain the central restriction

\[
 \boxed{\quad E(p(m))>m\log2-C\qquad(m\ge1).\quad}           \tag{3}
\]

An explicit weaker constant is \(C=\log x_0+640\). This is a condition
on the maximum **cumulative** excess before time \(p(m)\). No maximum over
all possible starting positions of a block is required.

The congruence and finite catalog collision steps are kernel-checked for
positive integers in [`CollatzRepetition.lean`](CollatzRepetition.lean).
The passage through (1)–(3) and the limits below remains a written proof.

The classical universal version uses
\(x_t<(3/2)^t(x_0+1)\). Dubickas's Theorem 5 and its Section 5 proof
give \(\liminf p(m)/m\ge\log2/\log(3/2)\) for a divergent positive
orbit. The refinement here keeps the actual cumulative odd count in that
collision argument. See [Dubickas, *On integer sequences generated by
linear maps*, Glasgow Mathematical Journal 51 (2009), 243–252](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C40C0C07FEC20797475BB2899C436C9A/S0017089508004655a.pdf/on_integer_sequences_generated_by_linear_maps.pdf).

## 3. The density theorem, with the correct limits

Define

\[
 \delta_c=\frac{\log2}{\log3},\qquad
 \delta_+=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{J_t}{t},\qquad
 \gamma=\delta_+\log3-\log2.
\]

Since \(D(t)\to\infty\),
\(\liminf J_t/t\ge\delta_c\), so \(\gamma\ge0\).
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the definition of limsup gives a constant
\(B_\varepsilon\) such that, for **all** \(T\ge0\),

\[
 E(T)\le(\gamma+\varepsilon)T+B_\varepsilon.                \tag{4}
\]

Indeed the same bound holds for \(D(t)\), after absorbing its finite
initial prefix; taking a maximum preserves it because
\(\gamma+\varepsilon>0\). Combining (3)–(4) proves

\[
 \frac{p(m)}m>
 \frac{\log2-(C+B_\varepsilon)/m}{\gamma+\varepsilon}.
\]

**Theorem 1.** If \(\delta_+>\delta_c\), then

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{p(m)}m
 \ge\frac{\log2}{\delta_+\log3-\log2}.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{5}
\]

If \(\delta_+=\delta_c\), then instead

\[
 \boxed{\quad p(m)/m\longrightarrow\infty.\quad}            \tag{6}
\]

For (6), let \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) in the preceding inequality.
This rules out even a bounded subsequence of \(p(m)/m\). An upper density
strictly below \(\delta_c\) is impossible. All three statements also
hold if a fixed finite prefix is removed.

If \(K=\liminf p(m)/m<\infty\), an equivalent form of (5) is

\[
 \delta_+\ge\delta_c\left(1+\frac1K\right).                \tag{7}
\]

The bound is non-strict. At equality the exponential rates balance, and
the uncontrolled constants and lower-order terms in (3) matter.

Summability is stronger than needed for (5): nonrepetition alone gives
\(x_t\to\infty\), hence \(\log P_t=o(t)\) by averaging
\(\log(1+1/(3x_t))\to0\). Bounded correction is needed for the useful
constant error in (3), especially the subexponential refinements below.

**Limits that cannot be interchanged.** A low value of \(J_T/T\) at some
large times does not bound the earlier maximum \(E(T)\). One can construct
binary schedules by alternating increasingly dominant runs of ones with
zeros, stopping each zero run when \(D(T)\) has fallen to approximately
\(\sqrt T\). These have \(D(T)\to\infty\), lower odd density
\(\delta_c\), and upper odd density 1. Along the zero-run endpoints,
the earlier maximum remains proportional to \(T\), although
\(D(T)/T\to0\). This is an example of a binary schedule, not a claim
that an integer orbit realizes it. It shows why a **liminf** density cannot
replace \(\delta_+\) in this proof.

## 4. Subexponential excess forces much larger complexity

Equation (3) can be stronger than the linear density bound. If an eventually
valid cumulative upper bound has the form
\(D(t)\le F(t)+B\), with \(F\) increasing, then

\[
 F(p(m))\ge m\log2-O(1).                                   \tag{8}
\]

Examples, with constants independent of \(t,m\), are:

* If \(D(t)\le a\log(t+1)+B\), \(a>0\), then
  \(p(m)+1\ge c\,2^{m/a}\) for some \(c>0\).
* If \(D(t)\le A t^\theta+B\), \(A>0\), \(0<\theta<1\), then
  \(p(m)\ge((m\log2-O(1))/A)^{1/\theta}\) for sufficiently large \(m\).
* If \(D(t)=o(t^\theta)\), \(0<\theta\le1\), then
  \(p(m)/m^{1/\theta}\to\infty\).

The first statement can be expressed using the factor entropy
\(h=\liminf_m\log p(m)/m\):

\[
 h\ge\frac{\log2}{a}.                                      \tag{9}
\]

Thus a nonrepeating positive orbit cannot simultaneously have a
zero-entropy parity word, \(\log p(m)=o(m)\), and a polynomial upper
bound \(x_t=O((t+1)^a)\) of any finite degree. This excludes a joint class
much larger than individual Sturmian words. It does not assert that every
itinerary has zero entropy. The universal binary bound \(p(m)\le2^m\)
by itself yields only \(a\ge1\); packing can independently strengthen
the polynomial-growth restriction.

Similarly, critical cumulative odd density excludes every itinerary with
\(\liminf p(m)/m<\infty\), regardless of the particular source of its
low complexity. Neither critical density alone nor superlinear complexity
alone leads to a contradiction.

## 5. A uniform substitution family as a concrete consequence

Consider any binary substitution of constant length \(L\ge2\) whose
two images each contain exactly \(r\) ones. Any infinite fixed point has
density \(r/L\). If \(L^k\) is the least power at least \(m\), every
length-\(m\) factor lies in the image of an adjacent pair, at one of
\(L^k\) offsets. Consequently

\[
 p(m)\le4L^k<4Lm.                                           \tag{10}
\]

Assume the exact integer inequalities

\[
 \boxed{\qquad 2^{4L}<3^{4r}<2^{4L+1}.\qquad}               \tag{11}
\]

The density is supercritical, so a positive integer realization could not
be an eventual cycle. But (5) would require a complexity coefficient
strictly larger than \(4L\), contradicting (10). Hence no positive
integer orbit has such a fixed point, a shift of it, or such a tail as its
parity itinerary.

This includes \(L=11,r=7\), because

\[
 17{,}592{,}186{,}044{,}416=2^{44}
 <3^{28}=22{,}876{,}792{,}454{,}961
 <2^{45}=35{,}184{,}372{,}088{,}832.
\]

Thus the exclusion covers every length-11 binary substitution with seven
ones in each image that possesses an infinite fixed point, with no need to
classify its individual factors. For example one can take
\(1\mapsto11111110000\), \(0\mapsto01111111000\).

For an **aperiodic** fixed point, the same conclusion extends to every
odd-denominator rational candidate. In the fixed-denominator numerator
model of `STURMIAN-ATTEMPT.md`, each aligned block has map
\(X\mapsto aX+bd/2^L\), \(a=3^r/2^L>1\), with one of two fixed
nonnegative integers \(d\). Therefore \(|X_t|\le C a^{t/L}\).
The rational collision criterion applies because
\(a^4<2\), by (11). It forces repetition, contradicting aperiodicity.
That extra hypothesis matters for rational candidates: negative rational
cycles with periodic words are not excluded by the positive-cycle density
inequality.

## 6. The exact remaining gap

Packing gives bounded correction and restrictions on the set of visited
integers. It does not give an upper bound on \(p(m)\). Density converts
cumulative growth into the denominator in (5); it does not bound the
complexity of every possible itinerary.

An itinerary could still satisfy (3) by having sufficiently rapid cumulative
growth, sufficiently large factor complexity, or both. In particular,
critical density with superlinear or exponential factor complexity survives
these restrictions. No theorem here forces every positive orbit to violate
(3), (5), or (8), and nontrivial cycles remain a separate unresolved issue.
